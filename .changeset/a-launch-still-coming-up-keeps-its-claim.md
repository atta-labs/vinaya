A launch that is still coming up when the start wait ends is reported as starting and keeps its claim, never as a failed start. `task_start` and `task_resume` launch a run detached and wait, bounded, for its driver lock; until now a wait that ran out was reported as an infrastructure failure and the request's claim was released — even though the launched process was still alive. On a real repository that is the normal case, not the exceptional one: `task run` renders and posts the frozen brief and runs its start-of-run sweep, both forge-bound, before the loop writes any lock. The Operator was told its start had failed while its developer was coming up, and the obvious next thing to do — call `task_start` again — would have put a second developer on one branch. The wait now has three outcomes, and only one is a failure: the lock appeared (confirmed), the process exited or never spawned (a failed start, carrying that run's own captured stderr, and the only outcome whose claim is released), or the wait ran out with the process still alive (a started run, whose claim is kept). Raising the wait would have fixed nothing, because no fixed wait bounds a slow forge or a large sweep. A claim now also records the pid of the process its own launch spawned, so the supersede path asks that process first: a live pid with no driver lock yet is a run whose preparation is outlasting the wait, never a dead claim to relaunch, while a claim whose process has exited and whose task has no driver is still superseded exactly as before.
